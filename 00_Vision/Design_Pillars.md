# Design Pillars

## Tiny Champions, Big Threat

Kobolds are small, expressive, and funny, but not harmless. They are dangerously competent miners, engineers, scavengers, and tunnel fighters. They can be cute by accident while still being genuinely threatening in a match.

## Every Combatant Had a Job

The key design question for creeps, beasts, constructs, and champions is:

> What job did this thing perform five minutes before the kobolds decided to send it into battle?

Lane units should feel repurposed from underground life: rat scavengers, mole bruisers, drill carts, rubble carriers, explosive haulers, crystal handlers, and other working creatures or tools pressed into war.

## Candle Culture Is Functional

Candles are not decoration only. They are ritual safety equipment, social identity, and threat detection. The flame reacts to bad air, cursed tunnels, Shinies, mycelial dead zones, and the Mouth Below.

Candles can appear on helmets, backpacks, saddles, carts, staffs, shrine-hooks, banners, shoulder rigs, lantern frames, and creature harnesses.

## Comedy Over Horror, Horror Under Comedy

The surface tone is energetic and absurd: overconfident kobolds, improvised weapons, saturated colors, and big expressions. The deeper layer is eerie: the Deep watches, flames behave wrongly, and the hidden mycelium may be shaping life underground.

## Readable From Above

The game must read well from a gameplay camera. Silhouettes, weapons, candles, class roles, team colors, and threat states should be clear at a glance.

## Distinct From Warcraft and D&D

Kobolds are folklore-rooted and fair game as a creature concept, but Candleguard must avoid copying specific Blizzard or D&D expressions. The candle motif should be systemic, cultural, and functional, not a one-candle head gag or catchphrase.
